The problem

Mail arrives faster than the desk can sort it.

A solo doctor or a small Seattle practice already runs the day with a short front desk. Patient email does not wait for a spare hour.

  • After-hours messages

    A scheduling note, a refill, or a parent message can land at 9pm. It waits until someone opens the inbox in the morning.

  • The inbox piles up

    Appointments, refills, records, billing, and vendor mail land in the same place. Between patients, the pile gets longer.

  • A small front desk

    The same people answer the phone, check patients in, and clear email. Sorting every message is the work that slips.

The inbox

What it sorts

It reads the practice inbox and separates the mail your team already handles, instead of leaving every request in one thread.

  • Appointment requests

    New visits, follow-ups, and reschedules, pulled out of the general inbox.

  • Refill requests

    Medication refill messages, grouped for the person who handles them.

  • Records requests

    Chart copies, forms, and records requests, set aside from day-to-day mail.

  • Billing

    Payments, statements, and insurance questions, routed toward billing.

  • Vendor and spam

    Sales mail and noise, kept out of the patient queue.

Anything urgent is flagged for staff. It drafts a reply for the team to approve, and it routes each message by what it is. It triages and drafts only. It gives no medical advice. Staff review everything before it goes out.
